Questions

Hair loss, answered.

Shedding, thinning, minoxidil, finasteride: what they mean and where Klara fits.

Who is Klara for?

For women and men who notice more hair in the shower or on the brush, a wider parting or thinning at the crown, and want a daily scalp routine without medicines.

How is Klara different from minoxidil and finasteride?

Minoxidil (sold as Regaine or Rogaine) and finasteride (Propecia) are medicines for androgenetic alopecia and are used under medical advice. Klara is cosmetic scalp care: a peptide shampoo and treatment with no minoxidil, no finasteride and no hormones. If you already use either, ask your doctor before adding anything new.

What do anagen and telogen mean?

Every hair cycles through a growth phase (anagen), a short transition (catagen) and a resting phase (telogen), after which it sheds. Losing 50 to 100 hairs a day is normal. Stress, illness or giving birth can push many hairs into telogen at once, which shows up months later as heavy shedding, known as telogen effluvium.

How does Klara compare with other hair loss shampoos?

Many well-known hair loss shampoos are built around one active ingredient: caffeine in Alpecin, aminexil in Vichy Dercos. Klara is built on NIBEC’s own patented peptides from regenerative research, and pairs the shampoo with a treatment you leave on for a few minutes.

When will I see results?

Hair grows about one centimetre a month, so give any scalp routine at least three months of daily use. [NIBEC study: what changed, and after how many weeks]

Can I use Klara with other hair loss treatments?

Klara contains no minoxidil or finasteride, so it can be part of a wider routine. If you use a prescription treatment, check with your doctor first. [NIBEC to confirm guidance for pregnancy and breastfeeding]
